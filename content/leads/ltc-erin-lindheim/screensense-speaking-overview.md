# ScreenSense Speaking Engagement — Student Data Privacy Overview

**For:** ScreenSense / Lauren  
**Audience:** Parents and school leaders in Marin County  
**Tone:** Accessible, practical, not policy-wonk

---

Frank Lucido has spent his career inside California school districts' data infrastructure — the systems that track everything from attendance and grades to special education plans and state reporting. Through Lucido Technology Consulting, he works directly with districts on SIS migrations, CALPADS compliance, and the legal frameworks that govern student data: FERPA, AB 1584, and California's pupil data contract requirements. This isn't abstract policy work — it's hands-on, in-the-weeds infrastructure where a single misconfigured integration can expose thousands of student records. Frank knows where student data actually lives, how it moves between systems, what vendors do with it, and — critically — what parents and schools have the right to demand.

ScreenSense already does essential work helping families navigate screen time, social media, and phones. But there's a parallel conversation most parents and schools haven't had yet: when a district adopts a new edtech tool or AI platform, where does your child's data actually go? Who owns it? What happens when it leaves the school network? And what rights do you have under the law? This talk bridges the gap between the ScreenSense mission — keeping tech in check so kids thrive — and the infrastructure reality that determines whether student data is protected or exposed. Drawing on real district implementations, privacy frameworks developed with colleagues like Jim Siegl (former FPF Senior Fellow and co-author of the CoSN privacy toolkit), and California-specific regulations that apply to every Marin County school, Frank offers parents and educators a clear, actionable understanding of student data privacy: the risks, the rights, and what to ask of your school.

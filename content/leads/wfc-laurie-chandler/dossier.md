This is not a generalist CBT practice; it's a niche, intellectually rich approach that attracts clients seeking meaning and transformation, not just symptom relief.

Her Resources page features Mary Oliver, Wendell Berry, and crisis resources — the curation reflects someone who thinks in poetry and symbol, not bullet points.

Her Psychology Today profile is well-maintained and substantive. Her own website is not.

## Discovery Questions — Wednesday 6/17 Call

### Lauren Leads (Relationship + Practice — ~15 min)

These are relationally oriented. Lauren should lead with warmth and curiosity.

1. **"Tell me about your practice — what kind of clients do you love working with most?"**
   *Surfaces her ideal client profile. This tells us who the website needs to speak to.*

2. **"What drew you to depth psychology and Jungian work? That's a distinctive path."**
   *Validates her expertise. Builds rapport. The answer becomes website copy.*

3. **"How do clients usually find you now? Psychology Today? Word of mouth?"**
   *Surfaces the referral pipeline. Reveals what's working and what's missing.*

4. **"What's the experience you want someone to have when they land on your website?"**
   *Gets her to articulate the feeling/impression — not features, but emotional impact. This is the design brief.*

5. **"Is there anything about your current site that you actually like or want to keep?"**
   *Shows respect for what exists. Surfaces attachments — maybe she loves the Mary Oliver poem or the horse image. Don't assume everything needs to go.*

### Frank Leads (SEO/GEO + Website — ~10 min)

Wait for Lauren to set the relational tone, then transition naturally.

6. **"When someone in Marin searches for 'Jungian therapist' or 'depth psychology therapist,' do you show up?"**
   *Rhetorical — she won't. Leads into the SEO conversation without being critical. "Let me show you what I mean" — pull up a quick search.*

7. **"Your Psychology Today profile is actually well put together — it's doing more work than your website right now. Have you noticed that?"**
   *Compliments the PT profile while surfacing the website gap gently. "What if your website worked as hard as your PT profile does?"*

8. **"We've found that therapists with a distinctive approach — like yours — actually have an SEO advantage. Most therapist sites all say the same thing. Yours doesn't have to."**
   *Frames her niche as an asset, not a liability. "Depth psychology" + "Jungian" + "Marin County" are low-competition, high-intent keywords.*

9. **"If we rebuilt your site, what would 'better' look like six months from now?"**
   *Vision question. Lets her imagine the outcome. Surfaces whether she wants more clients, different clients, or just a site that doesn't embarrass her.*

### Closing Questions

10. **"What's your timeline? Is this a 'someday' thing or something you want to move on this summer?"**
    *Qualification. If she says "someday," this is a nurturing conversation. If she says "I need this fixed," it's a pipeline lead.*

11. **"Who else would need to be involved in this decision, or is it just you?"**
    *Confirms she's the sole decision-maker. No surprises later.*

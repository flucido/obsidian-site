## 5-Minute Brief (Read Before Meeting)

**The person:** Jim Siegl, CIPT. Senior Fellow, Youth & Education Privacy at Future of Privacy Forum. For nearly 20 years, Technology Architect at Fairfax County Public Schools (180K students, 10th largest US district). Co-author of the CoSN Privacy Toolkit and Trusted Learning Environment seal program — the gold standards for K-12 data privacy. MS in IT from UVA. Recently published practitioner guides on Privacy Enhancing Technologies (PETs) for education. Based in DC.

**The context:** LinkedIn connection. Jim is a recognized expert on K-12 student data privacy. He moved from practitioner (FCPS) to policy (FPF) and now influences national privacy standards. Frank reached out to schedule a conversation.

**The play:** This is a peer exchange between two people who care about getting student data right — Frank from the implementation side (SIS migrations, CALPADS, data pipelines with CA districts) and Jim from the policy/framework side (FPF, CoSN toolkit, PETs). Find common ground. Share perspectives. Assess whether an ongoing relationship is valuable.

**Why this matters to LTC:**
- Every LTC contract has FERPA/AB 1584/SOPIPA requirements. Jim works at the policy level on exactly these issues.
- He knows district technology at scale from 20 years inside FCPS.
- He has a national view of where privacy policy is going — early signal for LTC.
- California's privacy landscape (SOPIPA, AB 1584) has nuances that national policy doesn't always capture. Frank can offer ground-level perspective; Jim can offer the national view.

**What NOT to do:**
- Don't ask for anything — no favors, no intros, no referrals on first call
- Don't pitch LTC services — he's not a buyer
- Don't over-defer — you're a practitioner with real implementation experience. He values that.

## Discussion Flow (Suggested)

### Opening — Why We're Here (3 min)
> "Thanks for connecting. I've been following your work — the PETs guides, what you've been doing around student data privacy. I wanted to reach out because privacy isn't a compliance layer for what I do, it's the foundation. Everything I build for California districts has FERPA, AB 1584, and SOPIPA baked in from the start. I work with districts on data infrastructure — SIS migrations, CALPADS pipelines. I'd love to compare notes."

### Shared Ground — His FCPS Experience (10 min)
> "Twenty years at Fairfax County — that's one of the largest, most complex districts in the country. What did you learn about data infrastructure at that scale that surprised you? What patterns did you see repeat across districts?"

This shows you understand the scale of his experience. It invites him to share war stories — practitioners love this.

> "When you were at FCPS, what was the hardest privacy problem to solve — not the policy, but the implementation? The thing that looked clean on paper but was messy in practice?"

This bridges his policy work with his practitioner past. Gets to real insight.

### California vs. National Landscape (10 min)
> "Most privacy policy work feels DC/East Coast-centric. California has its own dynamics — AB 1584, CALPADS, CDE. How does FPF think about state-level variation? Are there California-specific privacy challenges you see that aren't getting enough attention?"

Positions Frank as the California expert. Invites Jim's national perspective.

> "I'm in the weeds with districts doing SIS migrations — moving from one system to another, converting years of student data. Privacy isn't theoretical in that context. What pitfalls do you see most often in large-scale data migrations that districts don't anticipate?"

Specific, grounded question. Shows Frank's expertise while asking for Jim's insight.

### The Future — PETs and Beyond (5 min)
> "Your PETs practitioner guides just came out. For someone doing hands-on data infrastructure work with districts, what should I be paying attention to? What's coming that isn't obvious from the outside?"

Positions Frank as forward-looking. Shows he's tracking Jim's current work.

### Staying Connected (5 min)
> "I'd love to stay in touch. I think there's value in a practitioner perspective feeding into the policy conversation — and I'd certainly benefit from staying current on where the privacy landscape is heading. What does that look like to you?"

Open-ended. Let Jim define what an ongoing relationship could be.

**Subject:** North Bridge — iPad management, Wi-Fi, and safety

---

Shannon,

It was great to meet you yesterday — thank you for the time. I've organized my thinking around the three areas we discussed. These are initial thoughts — I'd want to look at your current setup before pointing at any specific path.

**1. iPad Management — First Priority**

Managing iPads one-by-one isn't sustainable as the fleet grows. Mosyle looks like a strong fit for a school your size — it's purpose-built for K-12 (25,000+ schools use it) and was designed education-first, not a business tool adapted for schools.

Here's what I've confirmed about what it can do:

- **One-to-one deployment:** Mosyle supports zero-touch setup via Apple Business Manager. Devices enroll automatically — no manual configuration per device. This lines up with what you'd want for an August rollout.
- **Pricing:** They offer a **free tier** ($0, no device limit) that covers full Apple device management for iPads — shared devices, single sign-on. For a school of 68, this may cover your basic needs. The **Premium tier** ($5.50/device/year — about $0.46/month) adds classroom management tools, automated workflows, and screen sharing. The **OneK12 tier** ($9/device/year) adds encrypted web filtering and endpoint security. For ~30 iPads, that's $165–270/year total.
- **Classroom management:** Teachers set class preferences once, deploy to all devices with one click. No IT involvement needed day-to-day.

I understand North Bridge already has a Mosyle account in place; what licensing tier are you currently on?

With the rest of the iPad rollout paused until August, we have a clean window to sort out the configuration before devices hit classrooms.

**2. Wi-Fi — Assessment First**

You described the current Wi-Fi as slow and unreliable. Before recommending hardware, I'd want to do a site walk — map dead zones, test throughput, check the existing AP placement and backhaul. That gives us a concrete report with options at different budget levels, not a guess. I can do the walk on a day that works for your schedule.

**3. Student Internet Safety**

GoGuardian is the name most schools know, but Mosyle's OneK12 tier includes encrypted web filtering that covers content categories, threat protection, and CIPA compliance — all built in, no separate subscription. If we go with the OneK12 tier, GoGuardian may be redundant. If we stay on the free or Premium tier for device management, GoGuardian (or a standalone filter) would still be needed. I'll lay out both paths side-by-side so you can see what's covered and at what cost.

---

**My recommendation for next steps:**

I'd suggest we start on a project basis — iPad setup and configuration, plus a Wi-Fi assessment — with clear deliverables so you know what you're getting. If down the road it makes sense to have someone managing this stuff month-to-month, we can talk about that. But let's solve what's in front of us first.

1. I get a look at the current Mosyle configuration and licensing — understand what's already in place
2. I schedule a site walk for the Wi-Fi assessment
3. You review the device management and filtering options over the next couple of weeks
4. We decide on scope and timing — with a clean runway to August for the full iPad rollout

No rush on the review. I know you have a school to run. I'll follow up next week to check in, but reach out anytime if questions come up.

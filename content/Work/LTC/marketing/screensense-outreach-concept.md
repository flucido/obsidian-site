## What Is ScreenSense?

ScreenSense (screensense.org) is a local Bay Area / Marin organization that teaches parents, caregivers, and schools how to be smart about how kids use computers and digital technology. They provide:
- Online resources and curated content
- Community-based dialogue
- Strategic guidance for schools on managing digital tech

Erin Lindheim has been in contact with them. The topics they cover — AI, safety, how kids interact with technology — overlap with what I've been working on.

---

## What I Want to Pitch

A talk or workshop for ScreenSense's parent/school audience on **data sovereignty and privacy** — not the usual "be careful online" content, but the deeper question:

**"Where does your child's data actually go?"**

Most parents think about screen time and content filters. Very few think about:
- What happens to their child's assessment data, attendance records, and learning analytics when it leaves the school network
- Who owns the data — the school, the vendor, the parent, the student?
- What "local-first" means and why it matters: keeping data on systems the school controls instead of shipping it to cloud services
- How privacy-enhancing technologies work — in plain English, not technical jargon

---

## The Angle

Three things I can bring that most presenters can't:

1. **I've actually built the systems.** I'm not theorizing about student data privacy — I've managed SIS migrations, CALPADS compliance, and data pipelines for California school districts. I know where the data goes because I've moved it.

2. **I have an interactive tool that makes this tangible.** A colleague (Jim Siegl, former Senior Fellow at the Future of Privacy Forum, co-author of the CoSN privacy toolkit) published an interactive guide to privacy-enhancing technologies — differential privacy, synthetic data, secure computation. It's designed for non-technical audiences. Parents could actually play with it and understand what "protecting student data" means in practice. See: privacyishard.net/pets/

3. **The local-first framing.** I've built an open-source working model for local-first data analytics in education — intentionally exploratory, designed to show what's possible when schools keep their data on their own infrastructure. This is a tangible demonstration of data sovereignty, not a lecture about it.

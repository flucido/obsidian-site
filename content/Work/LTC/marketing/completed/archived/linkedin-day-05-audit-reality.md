# Day 5 LinkedIn Post — The Audit Reality

Page 14 of your standard "SOPIPA-compliant" DPA contains a clause that voids itself the moment the vendor is acquired by private equity.

Read it. It's the assignment clause — and most of them say the vendor can assign the agreement to any successor entity without district consent.

Here's why this matters in 2026.

Northern California county auditors — Marin, Sonoma, SF, Contra Costa — are getting sharper. They're no longer accepting a vendor's compliance dashboard as evidence. They want code-level access logs. Deterministic query tracking. A verifiable chain of custody from raw SIS ingest to the graduation KPI on the board report.

When a vendor gets acquired, three things happen: the DPA's corporate signatory disappears, the data processing location might move, and the compliance posture the district vetted no longer exists. The new parent company's data practices — which the district never reviewed — now govern student PII.

Page 14 turned your audit defense into a voided contract.

When did your district last verify the corporate ownership chain of every signed DPA? If the answer is "at procurement" and that was four years and two acquisitions ago, you don't know who legally controls your student data.

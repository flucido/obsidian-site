# Day 3 LinkedIn Post — Data Tenancy vs. Data Sovereignty

Most superintendents believe their district owns its student data. Read clause 7.2 of your DPA.

A Data Owner controls the infrastructure. The code. The access logs. The model weights if AI is involved. When the vendor contract ends, the pipeline doesn't disappear — because the pipeline was never the vendor's.

A Data Tenant pays for access to records they already possess. Student PII flows to external APIs. When the contract ends, the data is returned in a format only the deprecated system can read. When the vendor gets acquired by private equity — and this happens, ask PowerSchool districts — the roadmap vanishes overnight.

The distinction isn't legal. It's architectural.

Three questions:

→ If your historical attendance data is trapped in a proprietary format only one vendor can read — tenant or owner?

→ If your analyst can open a terminal and query ten years of enrollment in under a second on local hardware — tenant or owner?

→ Has anyone successfully exported every record from a deprecated SIS in under 30 days?

Data sovereignty isn't a product from a conference booth. It's an architectural decision your district makes once. The moment you sign a DPA without reading clause 7.2, you've already made it — just not the one you think.

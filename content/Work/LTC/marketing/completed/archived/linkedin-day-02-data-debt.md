# Day 2 LinkedIn Post — Data Debt

We keep calling it "data debt." It's actually procurement debt with a technical surface.

Here's what I mean.

A district buys a SIS in 2018. The platform handles attendance, grades, enrollment. The analytics module is "coming next quarter." It ships two years later. By then, the district has built 47 Excel workbooks, three Access databases, and a weekly ritual where an analyst exports CSVs on Wednesday night and cross-references them by hand for Thursday's board packet.

The vendor calls this "operational maturity." The district calls it "how we get reports done."

Five years in, those manual workarounds are the actual infrastructure. The SIS is just where data sleeps between Excel sessions. When the contract comes up for renewal, the district can't leave — not because the product is good, but because 47 workbooks and three Access databases are wired to its export format.

That's not data debt. That's procurement debt. The district bought a promise, and the interest compounds in analyst hours.

Here's the diagnostic: if your longest-tenured analyst quit tomorrow, how many reports would stop being produced?

Every PDF export is a small future bill, paid in analyst hours. Most districts are sitting on a balance sheet that doesn't show the liability.

# Day 4 LinkedIn Post — The Convenience Tax

Districts trade $50K a year and full data control for the promise of someone to yell at when things break. The yelling rarely produces a fix.

Here's what that trade actually looks like, measured over a five-year contract cycle:

Year 1: The demo is beautiful. The dashboard populates in real time. Your analysts stop pulling CSVs manually. You tell the board this was money well spent.

Year 3: The vendor ships a UI redesign. Your reporting workflows break. Support ticket filed. Estimated response: 5–7 business days. Your board meeting is Thursday. An analyst is back in Excel.

Year 5: The vendor announces a platform migration. Your historical data needs to be extracted, transformed, and reloaded into the new system. The vendor offers this as a professional services engagement. $18,000. Your analysts have already done half the work in Polars on a five-year-old laptop.

The convenience tax isn't the subscription. It's the accumulating operational debt hidden behind a login screen.

Convenience is the most expensive line item in your tech budget — it just isn't labeled.

# LinkedIn Post 3 — Data Tenants vs. Data Owners

**Source:** "Beyond the Proprietary Black Box" — doc #1, "Navigating the California Student Data Privacy Stack" — doc #3
**Funnel:** Awareness
**Cluster:** K-12 Data Sovereignty

---

Most school districts don't own their data.

They rent it.

---

Here's the difference between a Data Owner and a Data Tenant:

A **Data Owner** controls the infrastructure. The code. The model weights. The access logs. Nothing leaves the district's security perimeter. When the contract ends, the pipeline remains.

A **Data Tenant** pays a vendor for access to records they already possess. Sensitive student PII flows to external APIs. When the contract ends, the pipeline disappears. When the vendor gets acquired by private equity — and this happens — the roadmap vanishes overnight.

---

Ask yourself three questions about your district's data infrastructure:

If your historical attendance data is trapped in a proprietary format only one vendor can read — are you a tenant?

If your analyst can open a terminal and query ten years of enrollment data in under a second on local hardware — are you an owner?

If the vendor deprecates the product your entire reporting workflow depends on — what's your backup plan?

---

Data sovereignty isn't a product you buy from a booth at a conference.

It's an architectural decision your district makes once and benefits from permanently.

What does your infrastructure say about who actually controls your student records?

# Common Questions from District Leaders

> Answered by Frank Lucido, Lucido Technology Consulting.
> For use in discovery calls, proposals, and follow-up conversations.

---

## "What exactly do you do?"

I help districts get control of their data. That usually means one of three things: (1) your SIS isn't giving you the answers you need, (2) you're migrating to a new system and need someone to make sure nothing breaks, or (3) your teams are spending too much time on manual data work that should be automated. I've done this across multiple SIS platforms — Aeries, PowerSchool, Infinite Campus — and the pattern is always the same: the tool isn't the problem, the data flow is.

---

## "We already have an IT director. Why would we need you?"

Most IT directors are managing everything — networks, devices, helpdesk, security, and data. I focus specifically on the data layer: SIS configuration, state reporting, assessment integration, and the workflows that connect your systems. Think of me as someone who comes in, understands your specific data landscape, and builds the systems your IT director doesn't have time to build. I work alongside your team, not instead of them.

---

## "How do you handle student data privacy?"

Every engagement includes FERPA and AB 1584 compliance language from day one — not as an afterthought, but built into the contract and the work. I've established IT governance frameworks that meet FERPA, GDPR, and SOPIPA standards. I don't access student data I don't need, I document everything I do access, and I hand off clean audit trails when the engagement ends. I also maintain relationships with privacy experts in the field — people who literally wrote the industry-standard privacy frameworks.

---

## "What does a typical engagement look like?"

Most engagements follow a five-phase structure:

1. **Infrastructure Audit** — Where is your data today? What systems are you running? Where are the bottlenecks?
2. **Pipeline Architecture** — Design the data flows between your SIS, assessment platforms, state reporting, and any other systems.
3. **Data Governance** — Establish the rules: who owns what data, how quality is maintained, what compliance requirements apply.
4. **Analytics Stack** — Build the reporting layer so your teams can actually use the data.
5. **Compliance** — Ensure everything meets FERPA, state requirements, and any contractual obligations.

Each phase produces deliverables your team can act on. I don't disappear into a back room and hand you a PDF — I'm working with your people throughout.

## "How much does it cost?"

It depends on the scope. A focused engagement (SIS migration QA, compliance audit, or pipeline rebuild) typically runs 4-12 weeks. I scope based on what you actually need, not a fixed package. I'll give you a clear workplan and cost estimate before we start. I've saved districts $200K+ annually through vendor consolidation alone — the ROI on getting your data right is usually obvious.

## "We're in the middle of a migration. Is it too late to bring you in?"

No — in fact, migrations are the most common reason districts call me. Whether you're moving from Aeries to PowerSchool, PowerSchool to Infinite Campus, or any other combination, the risk is always in the data transfer: records that don't map cleanly, historical data that gets lost, compliance gaps that don't show up until CALPADS rejects your submission. I've done this enough times to know where the problems hide.

---

## "What about CALPADS and state reporting?"

CALPADS is the backbone of California district data. If your CALPADS submissions aren't clean, everything downstream suffers — funding, accountability, compliance. I've delivered 100% accuracy on CALPADS submissions through automated validation workflows that catch issues before submission, not after. I also handle ELPAC, CAASPP, and STAR assessment coordination.

---

## "Do you work with English Learner data specifically?"

Yes — EL program data is one of the most complex data workflows in any district. Identification, placement, progress monitoring, reclassification, RFEP monitoring — every step involves multiple systems and compliance requirements. I've managed ELPAC testing administration and EL program data workflows, and I've built the integrations that ensure EL student data flows accurately between your SIS, the state reporting system, and the assessment platforms your educators depend on.

---

## "What's your experience with our SIS?"

I've worked primarily with Aeries (the most common SIS in California), but I'm familiar with PowerSchool, Infinite Campus, and Skyward. The specific tool matters less than understanding how your data flows through it. Every SIS has its own quirks — the question is whether someone on your team understands those quirks well enough to build reliable integrations around them.

---

## "How do we know this will work?"

I've done this before — across K-12 districts, international non-profits operating in 16 countries, and organizations managing $650M+ in assets. The approach is the same every time: understand the actual problem (not the symptom), build the solution alongside your team, and hand off something that works when I leave. I also build open-source working models — exploratory tools that let you see how data flows through a district, what transformations matter, and what's possible with tools you can run on a laptop. They're designed less as finished products and more as a way to engage with the ideas before committing to anything.

---

## "What's the LFED project I've heard about?"

LFED is an exploratory project I built to demonstrate something: district staff should be able to ask questions about their own data in plain English — no SQL, no technical training, no waiting for someone else to run a report. Ask "how many EL students reclassified last year by school?" and get an answer. It runs entirely on your infrastructure (no cloud AI services, no data leaving your network). I fine-tuned a language model specifically for this use case and published it open-source. It's intentionally exploratory — designed less as a finished product and more as a working model for how interactive, AI-supported tools might improve decision-making around complex data systems. The focus is on accessibility and intuition rather than technical completeness.
